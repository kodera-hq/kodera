# ADR-0004: Every infrastructure mutation is a job on a Postgres-backed queue

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Deciders:** the crew (proposal by zôÖma)

## Context
"Every control-plane mutation is a job with an audit row" is a launch rule. With a Redis-backed broker, the desired-state change, the audit row, and the enqueue are not atomic: a crash between commit and enqueue loses or duplicates the job. OpenTofu applies run 10–40 minutes; a worker restart mid-apply leaves locked state and a half-built project unless the job model records leases and checkpoints.

## Decision
- Scope: this decision reads that launch rule as applying to **infrastructure mutations**, as `docs/domain/JOB_CATALOGUE.md` does. An infrastructure mutation is a change to a cloud project and its IAM objects, a host, a network, a DNS record, a storage bucket, a mail-domain configuration, or an identity-realm object (the realm, its clients and its identity providers). Every infrastructure mutation is a job. Commercial effects (invoices, payment requests), messages (email, notifications) and user-level identity operations (invitations, enrolment, disabling a user) are not infrastructure and need not be jobs: they are durable outbox deliveries, committed with the change they belong to and executed by the worker. The worker loads the credential a delivery needs, such as the identity credential, for that delivery only, in the same way it loads credentials per job (ADR-0003, `docs/dev/SETTINGS_AND_SECRETS.md`). An ambiguous delivery outcome is reconciled, never retried automatically; FS-BILL-02 tests this for commercial effects. Other work, such as `export`, may still run as a job. Other control-plane changes commit with their own `AuditEvent`.
- Jobs live in **Postgres** (Procrastinate or an equivalent Postgres-backed queue), so `Job`, `AuditEvent`, and the desired-state change commit in **one transaction** (outbox pattern).
- `Job` fields: `id`, `tenant`, `kind`, `idempotency_key` (unique), `desired_spec_hash`, `status` in {queued, leased, running, succeeded, failed, orphaned, needs_reconciliation, cancelled}, `lease_expires_at` (renewed by the runner every 30 s), `fence` (strictly increasing), `attempt`, `steps[]` with per-step status and output reference, `unresolved_step` and `unresolved_operation_ref` (the step and the dispatched operation whose outcome is unknown), `reconciliation_decision_ref` (the operator decision that resolved it), `actor`, `parent_job`.
- `fence` takes a new, higher value on every claim, and renewing a lease keeps it. Step-state writes are conditional on the current fence. A command or provider call is dispatched only after the fence is checked atomically, in the same transaction that records the dispatch (`docs/specs/ENVIRONMENT_ALLOCATION.md`), so a stale runner can neither record a step nor start a new effect. The fence does not stop an operation already running: OpenTofu providers, Keycloak admin calls and SSH or Ansible cannot verify it, so such an operation is covered by the `needs_reconciliation` rule, not by the fence. A remote mutation through the Agent also needs the Agent itself to enforce the fence (ADR-0012).
- Steps are individually idempotent (tofu apply, Ansible role, Keycloak GET-then-PUT). A step that cannot complete, because the lease was lost or an effect's outcome is unknown, leaves the job in one of two states, decided from the dispatch records written with the fence check. A non-terminal sub-job counts as an open dispatch of its parent.
  - `orphaned`: no dispatch for the current step is open: none was recorded, or each has authenticated terminal proof. The job is safe to reclaim and re-runs automatically from the first non-succeeded step.
  - `needs_reconciliation`: a dispatch for the current step is recorded without terminal proof, or an effect's outcome is otherwise unknown. The job is **never recovered automatically**. It records the unresolved step and operation, alerts, and waits for an operator decision recorded with evidence (FS-CON-04, FS-CON-05). An expired lease, a stale heartbeat or an unheld advisory lock does not establish that the operation stopped; only authenticated terminal proof or a safe convergent outcome does. Which evidence counts is the open decision `external-execution-stop-evidence`. No `tofu force-unlock` runs without that recorded decision. The exits after that decision, and whether they count against `max_attempts`, are specified in `docs/domain/JOB_CATALOGUE.md`.
- **One OpenTofu state file per tenant** (`tenants/{slug}.tfstate`) with a per-tenant Postgres advisory lock.
- A mutation of a resource shared by an organisation's tenants, such as the organisation's one identity realm (ADR-0006), also takes a **per-organisation** Postgres advisory lock: a tenant lock cannot protect a shared realm. A change to an organisation-wide resource, such as creating or changing the organisation's realm, runs as its own job, not as a sub-job, and holds the organisation lock exclusively. Jobs that only depend on that resource, such as sibling provisions in one organisation, hold the organisation lock shared, so they run concurrently. An outbox delivery that changes an organisation's realm, such as a user-level identity operation, is not delivered while a job holds or has reserved that organisation's lock exclusively.
- A job acquires its whole lock set before its first step, in one fixed order: the organisation, then tenants, then any other lock class in the fixed order `docs/domain/JOB_CATALOGUE.md` defines, with ids sorted within each class. Each lock is shared or exclusive, in the mode that catalogue sets for the job kind; claims for a lock are served in the order they were requested, as that catalogue specifies, so an exclusive request is not starved by a stream of shared holders. A sub-job runs under its parent's locks and never acquires locks of its own: the parent's up-front set covers every lock, in the mode, that any sub-job it may start needs, and is held until every sub-job is terminal. No job waits on another job while holding locks, except a parent on its sub-jobs: an organisation-wide change a job needs is created before that job, and must be terminal before that job is claimed. Under this acquisition rule, two jobs cannot deadlock on these locks.
- While a runner is live, these locks are Postgres session advisory locks, and a database session that ends releases them. A lock set is therefore also reserved durably, not only by a database session, from the moment the job is claimed, and a claim checks these reservations as well as the live locks. A reservation ends when the job becomes terminal or `orphaned`. A job in `needs_reconciliation` keeps its lock set reserved, and no job needing a conflicting lock is claimed until the operator's decision is recorded. A parent's reservation covers its non-terminal sub-jobs.
- **Drift detection**: nightly `tofu plan -detailed-exitcode` and Ansible `--check` per tenant, stored on `Tenant.observed`.
- Upgrades follow: maintenance mode on → hot safety snapshot → pull → up → migrate → preflight → maintenance off; on a red preflight, restore the safety snapshot. The snapshot holds the database dump, the rendered configuration and the image and profile pins (`docs/specs/UPGRADE_PROCEDURE.md`); a restore returns those to the moment the snapshot was taken and discards everything written to them after it. Anything else, such as object-storage bodies and the organisation's realm, is not in the snapshot.

## Options considered
- Celery on Redis: rejected for the atomicity and long-running-subprocess problems above.
- Ad-hoc scripts run from a bastion: rejected as the normal operating model; allowed only for an explicitly approved pilot or break-glass operation that follows the manual runbook and records evidence.

## Consequences
- One more Postgres schema to migrate and monitor; no Redis in the control plane.
- Jobs and their `AuditEvent` rows are the audit trail for infrastructure mutations; other control-plane changes are audited by their own `AuditEvent`. Exports of `Job` and `AuditEvent` are evidence for the DPA's accountability clause, not the whole of it.
- Procrastinate's stalled-job recovery (`get_stalled_jobs` and `retry_job`, driven by worker heartbeats) is not used for jobs with external effects: a missing heartbeat does not prove that an effect stopped. The fence, `needs_reconciliation` and orphan recovery are built on top of the queue.
